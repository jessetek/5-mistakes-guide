What it really costs to own a home in Downey, taxes included

Property tax, the supplemental bill, insurance, HOA and repairs, explained by a Downey realtor who sells here every month.

By Jesse Oñate, Realtor · Updated November 25, 2026

The short version
  • At the $874K median price, the 1% base property tax is about $8,740 a year, or $728 a month, before voter approved charges.
  • Your tax resets to your purchase price, so never use the seller's tax number.
  • Expect a separate supplemental tax bill after closing, mailed to you and not paid from your impound account.
  • Get insurance quotes during escrow, and ask what any policy leaves out.
  • Ask your lender for a payment estimate that includes tax and insurance for the exact address.

Owning a home in Downey costs more than the mortgage payment, and the biggest add-on is property tax. My Downey market guide puts the median sale price around $874K, and the county base levy is 1% of assessed value, so a buyer at that price should plan on roughly $8,700 a year in property tax before voter approved charges. That is about $730 a month, and it is the number I see buyers forget most often.

Here is how I walk clients through the full cost of owning in Downey: property tax, the supplemental bill, insurance, HOA dues if you buy a condo, and the repairs an older house asks for. I sell homes here every month, so I will give you the real mechanics and tell you where to check your own numbers.

How much is property tax in Downey?

The Los Angeles County Auditor-Controller explains that a tax rate includes a general 1% levy that applies to every bill, plus voter approved debt for your particular area. The voter approved part is recalculated each year and changes as new bonds are added or old ones are paid off. So the honest answer is "a bit over 1%," and the exact figure depends on your address.

The county does not publish one citywide percentage for Downey. It has a Tax Rate Area lookup on the Auditor-Controller site, and I tell every buyer to run the address there before making an offer. In the meantime, here is the base 1% on a few Downey price points, using the typical values from my market guide.

These are the base levy only. Add the voter approved debt shown on your address's tax rate area, and any direct assessments on the bill. If you want to see how the tax changes the all in payment, put the numbers in my mortgage calculator and include the tax line.

Why will my tax bill be higher than the seller's?

Because California reassesses a home when it sells. The LA County property tax site says a property's base year value can rise by no more than 2% each year unless there is a change in ownership or new construction. A family that bought a Downey ranch house years ago may be paying tax on a value far below today's price. When you buy, the county resets the value to your purchase price.

That is why I never tell a buyer to use the seller's tax number from a listing. Use your own purchase price times 1%, then add the voter approved charges for the address.

What is a supplemental tax bill?

The county's own wording is that a supplemental assessment reflects the difference between the new assessed value and the old one. After you close, the Tax Collector sends a supplemental bill for that difference, in addition to the regular annual bill. It goes to you, the owner, even if your lender collects taxes in an impound account, and the county says these bills are generally not paid from the impound account.

Here is an example of how it works, using round numbers and not a real property. If the old assessed value was $300K and you buy at $874K, the gap is $574K. At the 1% base rate, the full year tax on that gap is about $5,740, prorated for the months you own the home in that fiscal year. Your bill will differ, because the exact proration depends on your closing date and the voter approved charges. Supplemental bills have their own due dates, printed on the bill, so budget the cash ahead of time and watch your mail after closing.

When are LA County property taxes due?

The annual bill has two installments. These are the dates from the LA County Tax Collector.

InstallmentDueLate afterPenalty
FirstNovember 1December 1010%
SecondFebruary 1April 1010% plus $10

If a delinquency date lands on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. If you close on a house and no bill reaches you, the county says you still owe the tax, and not receiving a bill does not cancel a late penalty. Ask the Tax Collector for a copy if it does not show up.

What about Mello Roos and special assessments?

Most of Downey is older housing stock, so special taxes are less common here than in newer master planned areas. But I still read the disclosures on every deal, because a direct assessment on the tax bill is part of the monthly cost. If you see one, my guide to Mello Roos and special taxes shows how to read it and how lenders count it.

How much is homeowners insurance in Downey?

I will not quote a premium, because it depends on the age of the house, the roof, the coverage and the insurer, and quotes change often. What I tell buyers to do is get two or three real quotes during escrow, before the contingency period ends, so the number is in your payment before you commit.

One thing to know. California runs the FAIR Plan as insurance for people who cannot get coverage from a regular insurer. According to the California Department of Insurance, the current residential FAIR Plan policy is a limited fire policy, and water damage, liability, theft and additional living expenses are not included. Downey is not a wildfire area the way the foothills are, so most of my buyers here use a regular insurer, but ask any agent or broker what the policy actually covers.

Earthquake coverage is separate. The California Earthquake Authority sells earthquake policies only through participating insurers. In Southern California I always ask buyers to price it and decide with eyes open. Whether you buy it is your call, and a licensed insurance agent can quote it for your address.

What are HOA dues on a Downey condo?

Dues vary a lot by complex, so I will not give you an average. What matters is how lenders treat them. HOA dues count in your debt to income ratio, so they reduce how much house you can qualify for. The condo tier in Downey sits near $543K, and that lower price comes with a monthly fee that a house does not have. Ask for the budget, the reserve study and the meeting minutes before you write an offer, and read them for any planned special assessment.

What should I budget for repairs on an older Downey house?

Much of Downey was built in the 1940s and 1950s, and that is the part of the cost nobody can look up. The things I watch in inspections are the sewer line, the plumbing, the electrical panel, the roof and the foundation. I do not put a dollar figure on a "typical" year, because it is not honest to. What I do suggest is keeping a cash cushion after closing and getting a sewer line scope during your inspection period. It is one of the cheapest ways to avoid a big surprise.

Can I keep my old tax base if I am 55 or older?

Possibly. The California Board of Equalization explains that under Proposition 19, homeowners who are 55 or older, severely and permanently disabled, or victims of a wildfire or governor declared disaster can transfer the base year value of their home to a replacement home anywhere in California. The replacement must be bought or built within two years of selling the old home, and the transfer can be used up to three times. The replacement home can cost more than the old one, but any value above the allowed threshold is added to the transferred base. The claim is filed with the assessor of the county where the replacement home is located.

If you are selling a long held home to move into Downey, this can change the math a lot. Run it by a CPA or attorney, and use my seller net sheet to see what you walk away with from the sale.

What is the real monthly cost to own in Downey?

Add up the monthly pieces: principal and interest, property tax, insurance, HOA if any, and a repair set aside. Of those, property tax is the most predictable. At the median price, the 1% base is about $728 a month before the voter approved charges. The rest depends on your loan, your down payment and your insurance quote. If you want a number built for your situation, my first time buyer guide for Downey lays out programs and budgets, and my guide to the best neighborhoods in Downey shows what each budget buys. For an example of a Downey deal done well, read how the Garcia family sold $32K over asking.

My advice: before you tour, ask a lender for a full payment estimate that includes tax and insurance for the address, not just principal and interest. A house that looks affordable at the listing price can stretch a budget once the tax and insurance are in. That one step prevents most of the stress I see after closing.

Questions people ask me

How much is property tax in Downey, CA?

The base is a general 1% levy on assessed value, plus voter approved debt for your tax rate area. At the $874K median price, the base alone is about $8,740 a year. Look up your address in the LA County Auditor-Controller Tax Rate Area lookup for the exact rate.

Why did my tax bill go up after I bought?

California reassesses a home when it changes hands. The new assessed value is based on your purchase price, so your bill is usually higher than the seller's. After that, the base value can rise by no more than 2% a year unless there is new construction or another sale.

What is a supplemental tax bill in LA County?

It is a bill for the difference between the new and old assessed value after a sale. It comes in addition to the regular annual bill, goes to the owner, and is generally not paid from an impound account. Its due dates are printed on the bill.

When are LA County property taxes due?

The first installment is due November 1 and late after December 10. The second is due February 1 and late after April 10. Late payments carry a 10% penalty, plus a $10 cost on the second installment.

Can I take my old property tax base to Downey?

If you are 55 or older, severely and permanently disabled, or a wildfire or disaster victim, Proposition 19 may let you transfer your base year value to a replacement home in California, up to three times, if you buy within two years of the sale. Talk to a CPA or attorney.
