SoCal mortgage rates: the real history

Every monthly 30-year and 15-year average for 2025 and 2026, straight from Freddie Mac, plus what each move does to your payment.

By Jesse Oñate, Realtor · Updated November 12, 2026

The short version
  • The 30-year fixed averaged 6.05% in February 2026, the low of the year.
  • It climbed to a 7.40% peak on October 8 and 15, 2026.
  • On November 12, 2026 it was 7.11%, with the 15-year at 6.45%.
  • On a $700K loan, the swing from 6.05% to 7.40% is more than $600 a month.

Here is the real SoCal mortgage rate history for 2025 and 2026. Mortgage rates in Southern California follow the national 30-year fixed average that Freddie Mac publishes every Thursday. In 2026 that rate started the year near 6.1%, bottomed out around 6.05% in February, then climbed for eight straight months to a peak of 7.40% in October. As of November 12, 2026 it sits at 7.11%. This page keeps every monthly average in one place, so you can see where rates have actually been before you decide when to buy, sell or refinance.

I update this history every month with Freddie Mac's official numbers. If you want the weekly number the morning it comes out, my Thursday rate text sends it to your phone with a one-line take on what it means for Downey-area buyers.

What happened to rates in 2026?

The year split cleanly in two. From January through March, the 30-year fixed hovered between 6% and 6.4%, with the single lowest week at 5.98% on February 26. Starting in April it rose almost every month: 6.33% in April, 6.49% in June, 6.67% in August, then a sharp jump in the fall to 6.86% in September and 7.33% in October. The single highest weekly reading of the year was 7.40%, on October 8 and October 15.

November has eased slightly, to 7.11% on November 12. One week is not a trend. Buyers I work with plan around the payment they can afford today, not around a guess about next month.

What does a rate change do to your payment?

Rate moves matter more than most people expect. On a $700,000 loan with a 30-year fixed rate, principal and interest alone works out to:

  • At 6.05% (February 2026 low): about $4,219 a month
  • At 6.49% (June 2026): about $4,420 a month
  • At 7.11% (November 12, 2026): about $4,709 a month
  • At 7.40% (October 2026 peak): about $4,847 a month

That is a swing of more than $600 a month on the same house within one year. Property tax, insurance and any HOA come on top. Run your own numbers in my mortgage calculator, which uses the live weekly rate.

Should you wait for rates to drop before buying?

Waiting has its own costs. Prices in my farm cities have held up, and when rates do fall, more buyers come back at once and competition picks up. My usual advice is to buy when the payment fits your budget and the right home shows up, then refinance later if rates fall far enough to make the math work. My refinance guide shows how to figure out when a refinance actually pays for itself.

If you are selling, rates shape your buyer pool. Higher rates mean fewer buyers can qualify at the top of your price range, so pricing it right in the first two weeks matters even more. My pricing guide explains why.

How to use this rate history

A rate history is most useful as a reality check. When someone tells you rates are "way up" or "finally coming down," look at the table and see how far they actually moved. A quarter point sounds small, but on a $700,000 loan it is roughly $115 a month.

It also helps you judge timing. If you locked in 2025 or early 2026 near 6%, you are well below today's market, so selling and buying again means taking on a higher rate. If you bought in late 2023 or 2024 at 7.5% or more, a refinance may already make sense, and my refinance guide walks through the break-even math.

Lastly, remember that the rate is only one part of the payment. In LA County, property tax usually runs about 1% plus voter approved bonds, and some neighborhoods carry Mello-Roos on top. My Mello-Roos guide shows how to check before you write an offer.
