Should you refinance your home in 2026?

How to find your break-even point, a worked example at today's rates and the cases where refinancing is the wrong call.

By Jesse Oñate, Realtor · Updated November 12, 2026

The short version
  • Freddie Mac's 30-year rate was 7.11% on Nov 12, 2026, so a refinance only helps if your current rate is clearly higher.
  • The real question is break-even: closing costs divided by monthly savings.
  • Example: refinancing a $547K balance from 7.85% to 7.11% saves about $270 a month, and a $9,000 cost takes about 33 months to repay.
  • If you may sell before your break-even date, skip it.

Should you refinance in 2026? Only if your current rate is clearly higher than today's and you will stay in the home long enough to earn back the closing costs. Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed average was 7.11% on November 12, 2026, down from a peak of 7.40% in October, so it is worth checking if you took your loan at the top of the market. For many homeowners it will not make sense yet.

I am a realtor, not a lender, and I earn nothing if you refinance. I am giving you the same math I give my own clients. Talk to a lender and a tax professional before you decide.

How do you know if a refinance is worth it?

Skip the old rule that says wait until rates drop a full point. The better test is your break-even point: the closing costs divided by the monthly savings. If you will keep the loan longer than that, a refinance can pay off. If you might sell sooner, it will not.

A worked example at today's rates

This is an illustration, not a quote. Say you took a $560,000 30-year loan at 7.85%, which puts your principal and interest payment at about $4,051. After 30 payments your balance is about $547,000. Assume $9,000 in closing costs, which is an example figure only. Real costs vary by lender and loan, and your Loan Estimate will show yours.

What if you are a buyer who plans to refinance later?

Some buyers tell me they will buy now and refinance when rates fall. That can work, but it is a bet, not a plan. Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 6.17% a year earlier and is 7.11% today, so a drop could come, but nobody can promise when. Pick a price where the payment works at today's rate. If rates fall and the break-even math works, a refinance is a bonus rather than the thing holding your budget together.

On a $600,000 loan, the payment is about $4,036 a month at 7.11% and about $3,792 at 6.50%, a gap of roughly $244. That is helpful, but it would not rescue a budget that was already stretched.

What should you gather before you call a lender?

  • Your current loan statement with the balance, rate and monthly payment.
  • Your home's estimated value. The lender will order an appraisal, but you can start with my free home value tool.
  • Quotes from three lenders on the same day, using the same loan amount, so you can compare the Loan Estimates line by line.
  • How long you plan to stay. This one answer decides whether the break-even math works.

When is refinancing the wrong call?

  • You might sell soon. If you will not reach break-even, you lose money on the costs.
  • Your rate is already close to today's. A small drop rarely covers the cost and the hassle.
  • You have a low rate from the last few years. Refinancing would raise your rate, so do not.
  • You are early in the loan. Some lenders have waiting periods, so ask.
  • Your equity is thin. Under about 20% equity, mortgage insurance can eat the savings.

What about a cash out refinance or a home equity loan?

If you are pulling cash out and your current rate is low, a second loan can beat a full refinance because it leaves your first mortgage alone. Ask a lender to compare both, and ask a CPA how the interest is treated for taxes.

Should you refinance or sell?

If you are thinking about moving up, the better question is what your home is worth and what you would pay next. Questions people ask me

What is a break-even point on a refinance?

It is your total closing costs divided by your monthly payment savings. If you will keep the loan and the home longer than that many months, the refinance can pay off.

What was the mortgage rate on November 12, 2026?

Freddie Mac's survey showed the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.11% and the 15-year fixed at 6.45% on November 12, 2026.

Is it worth refinancing if rates only drop a little?

Usually not. A small drop saves little per month, so the break-even point can run well past the time you plan to stay. Compare the Loan Estimate to your current payment.

Do you make money if I refinance?

No. I am a realtor, not a lender, so I have no financial stake in your refinance. Talk to a lender and a tax professional for your own numbers.
