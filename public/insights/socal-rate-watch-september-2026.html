SoCal mortgage rates: September 2026 recap

Mortgage rates climbed every week of September. Here are the real Freddie Mac numbers and what they did to a monthly payment.

By Jesse Oñate, Realtor · Updated November 12, 2026

The short version
  • Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed rate rose from 6.71% on Sept 3 to 7.03% on Sept 24, 2026.
  • The 15-year fixed rose from 6.04% to 6.42% over the same weeks.
  • On a $700K loan, the move from the end of August to Sept 24 added about $173 a month in principal and interest.
  • A year earlier, in September 2025, the 30-year rate was between 6.26% and 6.50%.

Mortgage rates went the wrong way in September 2026. According to Freddie Mac's weekly survey, the average 30-year fixed rate rose from 6.71% on September 3 to 7.03% on September 24. The 15-year fixed rose from 6.04% to 6.42% in the same stretch.

I wrote an earlier version of this page before the month happened and it did not match reality, so I rewrote it with the actual numbers. Everything below comes from Freddie Mac's published weekly history.

What were mortgage rates each week of September 2026?

Week30-year fixed15-year fixed
Aug 276.66%5.98%
Sept 36.71%6.04%
Sept 106.76%6.09%
Sept 176.95%6.26%
Sept 247.03%6.42%

The biggest single jump was the week of September 17, when the 30-year rate rose 0.19 points. By the end of the month it was above 7% for the first time in 2026. For comparison, the same weeks in September 2025 ran between 6.26% and 6.50%.

What does that do to a monthly payment?

On a $700,000 loan, a 30-year payment at 6.66% is about $4,498 a month in principal and interest. At 7.03% it is about $4,671. That is roughly $173 more every month, or about $2,076 a year, for the exact same house. Try your own numbers in my mortgage calculator.

How did September compare with the rest of 2026?

This is the part that surprised me. Freddie Mac's 30-year average started 2026 near 6.1% and drifted between roughly 6.0% and 6.5% through spring, hitting a low of 5.98% on February 26. By the end of August it was 6.66%. September then pushed it to 7.03%, a jump of 0.37 points in a single month. If you started shopping in the winter, the same loan costs noticeably more now.

On a $700,000 loan, the 5.98% low would have meant about $4,188 a month in principal and interest. The 7.03% rate is about $4,671, a difference of roughly $483 a month.

Should you consider a 15-year loan at these rates?

The 15-year rate is lower than the 30-year rate, but the payment is higher because you repay faster. On a $500,000 loan, the 30-year at 7.03% is about $3,337 a month, while the 15-year at 6.42% is about $4,334 a month. The 15-year costs about $997 more each month, but total interest drops from roughly $701,000 to roughly $280,000.

For most buyers in this part of LA County, the 15-year payment is too high to carry on a median priced home, so it is not for everyone. It is worth running both if you have a large down payment or you are refinancing with a lot of equity.

What is a rate lock and when should you use one?

A rate lock holds your quoted rate for a set number of days while your loan closes, usually 30 to 60 days. When rates are moving up week by week, as they did in September, locking sooner protects you from a further rise. If you lock and rates fall, ask your lender whether they offer a float down option before you sign. Terms vary, so get the details in writing.

What should buyers do after a rate jump?

I do not try to time rates, and I would not try to guess where they go next. Three things are worth doing:

  • Re-run your budget at the new rate. A pre-approval from August may no longer match the payment you are comfortable with.
  • Shop at least three lenders. Quotes on the same day can differ, and that gap is free money if you ask.
  • Plan to refinance later, not to wait. If you find the right home, you can buy now and refinance if rates fall, but do not count on it. Make sure the payment works today.

Questions people ask me

What was the 30-year mortgage rate in September 2026?

Freddie Mac's weekly average for the 30-year fixed was 6.71% on September 3, 6.76% on September 10, 6.95% on September 17 and 7.03% on September 24, 2026.

What was the 15-year mortgage rate in September 2026?

How much does a 0.37 point rate increase cost per month?

On a $700,000 30-year loan, moving from 6.66% to 7.03% adds about $173 a month in principal and interest.

Should I buy a home when rates are rising?

If the payment fits your budget today and you plan to stay for years, buying can still make sense. Re-run your budget at the current rate and talk to more than one lender before you decide.
