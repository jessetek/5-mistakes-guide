SoCal mortgage rates: October 2026 recap

Rates peaked at 7.40% in early October, then began to ease. The real Freddie Mac numbers, and what they mean for a payment.

By Jesse Oñate, Realtor · Updated November 12, 2026

The short version
  • Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed rate hit 7.40% on Oct 8 and Oct 15, 2026, then eased to 7.24% on Oct 29.
  • The 15-year fixed peaked at 6.73% on Oct 8 and ended the month at 6.60%.
  • On a $700K loan, 7.40% is about $573 a month more than the 6.17% rate of a year earlier.
  • Easing is not a trend yet. Plan around the payment you can afford now.

October 2026 started with the highest mortgage rates of the year and ended a little lower. Freddie Mac's weekly survey had the 30-year fixed rate at 7.28% on October 1, a peak of 7.40% on October 8 and 15, then 7.32% on October 22 and 7.24% on October 29. The 15-year fixed ended the month at 6.60%.

I wrote an earlier version of this page before the month happened and it was wrong, so I replaced it with the actual numbers from Freddie Mac's weekly history.

For context, the 30-year rate was between 6.17% and 6.34% in October 2025, so borrowers are paying more than a full point above where rates stood a year ago.

What does 7.40% do to a payment?

On a $700,000 30-year loan, a 6.17% rate is about $4,274 a month in principal and interest. At 7.40% it is about $4,847. That is roughly $573 a month more for the same home. The slight easing to 7.24% by October 29 trims about $76 a month from the peak. Use my mortgage calculator to test your own price and down payment.

How did October compare with the rest of 2026?

Looking at Freddie Mac's history, October's 7.40% peak is the highest weekly 30-year reading of 2026. The year began near 6.1%, dipped to 5.98% in late February and stayed between about 6.0% and 6.7% until September. On a $700,000 loan, 5.98% is about $4,188 a month in principal and interest and 7.40% is about $4,847, a difference of roughly $659 a month.

So the two weeks of easing at the end of October matter less than the bigger picture: borrowing costs are well above the first half of the year. That is the number to plan around.

Should you consider a 15-year loan?

The 15-year rate peaked at 6.73% on October 8. On a $500,000 loan, the 30-year at 7.40% is about $3,462 a month and the 15-year at 6.73% is about $4,419. You pay about $957 more each month, but total interest falls from roughly $746,000 to roughly $295,000. Most buyers cannot carry the higher payment, so treat it as an option to price out, not a goal.

What did rates do after October?

Freddie Mac's survey showed the 30-year rate at 7.18% on November 5 and 7.11% on November 12, so the easing that began in late October has continued for four straight weeks. I would not call that a trend until it lasts, and I would not wait for a number that may not come. If you are shopping now, build your budget on today's rate and treat any further drop as a bonus.

What should buyers do right now?

  • Buy the payment, not the rate. Pick a monthly number you can carry comfortably and work backward to a price.
  • Get quotes from three lenders. Rate and fee differences are real, and it costs nothing to ask.
  • Ask about buying down the rate. Sellers sometimes pay for it, and in a market where homes take around 45 days to sell, as my Downey market guide shows, there is room to ask.

If you are just starting, my buyer guide walks through the steps, and I can run your numbers on a short call.

How should you compare lender quotes?

Ask each lender for a Loan Estimate on the same day, for the same loan amount and the same lock period. Compare the rate, the points and the total of the fees, not just the headline rate. A quote that looks 0.125 points lower can cost more once the fees are added in, so line them up side by side before you choose.

What about sellers and refinancing?

Sellers should expect buyers to be payment sensitive. Pricing right on day one matters more than a price cut later. If you are thinking about refinancing, read my honest refinance guide to see when it does and does not make sense at current rates.

Questions people ask me

What was the 30-year mortgage rate in October 2026?

Freddie Mac's weekly average for the 30-year fixed was 7.28% on October 1, 7.40% on October 8 and 15, 7.32% on October 22 and 7.24% on October 29, 2026.

Did mortgage rates go down in October 2026?

They peaked at 7.40% in the second week and then eased. The month closed at 7.24%, slightly below the 7.28% where it started on October 1.

How much more is a payment at 7.40% than a year ago?

On a $700,000 30-year loan, 7.40% costs about $573 a month more in principal and interest than the 6.17% rate of late October 2025.

Is it a bad time to buy a home?

Only you can answer that from your budget. If the payment works at today's rate and you plan to stay several years, buying can still make sense. Get quotes from several lenders and run the numbers first.
