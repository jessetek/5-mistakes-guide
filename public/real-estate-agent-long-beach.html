For Buyers

Buying a home in Long Beach

Most Long Beach buyers lose time touring the wrong part of town. We pick the neighborhood first, then the house.

Match the area to your life

Walk to the beach and dinner? Belmont Shore. A yard and an older home with character? Bixby Knolls or California Heights. Close to Cal State Long Beach and El Dorado Park? Los Altos. We settle this on a 15-minute call before you burn a weekend.

Comps from your pocket only

I pull sales from the same neighborhood and the same kind of house. A Los Altos ranch is not a comp for a Belmont Heights bungalow, even if the square footage matches. That's how you offer enough to win without paying for the wrong data.

Older homes, full inspections

A lot of Long Beach housing is decades old, from Craftsman homes in Bixby Knolls to mid-century houses on the east side. I'll push for a sewer line scope and a real look at the foundation and roof, so surprises show up before you sign, not after.

For Sellers

Selling your Long Beach home

At roughly 42 days on market, Long Beach buyers take their time. The listings that move fast are priced right in week one.

Priced to your neighborhood

Not a Zestimate and not the citywide median. I price your home against what sold near you, in your condition, in the last few months. Overpricing in Long Beach usually means a price cut later and a lower final number.

Sell the location, not just the house

Buyers pay for the walk to Second Street, the A Line ride to downtown LA, a quick hop onto the 405, or a short drive to Cal State Long Beach. Your listing should say exactly why your spot works, in plain words.

Fix only what pays

Paint, landscaping and small repairs often earn their cost back. A full kitchen remodel right before listing often does not. I'll tell you which is which for your house before you spend a dollar.

Local Knowledge

Long Beach neighborhoods I cover

The 405 cuts Long Beach into a north half and a south half. The 710 runs down the west side, the 605 serves the southeast, and the 91 runs near the northern edge. Where you land on that map changes everything.

Bixby Knolls and California Heights: both grew out of the old Rancho Los Cerritos land after it was subdivided starting in 1916. Expect Craftsman and mid-century homes, mature trees, Virginia Country Club nearby, and Atlantic Avenue as the main strip for coffee and errands. Buyers here usually want character and a yard, and they will pay for original details in good shape.

Belmont Shore, Belmont Heights and Naples: the coastal side. Belmont Shore is about walkability and Second Street. Naples sits on canals around Alamitos Bay, and its waterfront homes are their own price tier. Lots are small and parking is tight, so I always check what comes with the house: garage, alley access, or street only.

Los Altos and the east side: mostly mid-century ranch homes and bungalows, close to Cal State Long Beach and El Dorado Regional Park, with its nature center. Good fit for families who want a quieter street and quick access to the 405 and 605.

North Long Beach: the area north of Bixby Knolls, around Houghton Park, close to the 91 and the 710. If you want into Long Beach on a tighter budget, this is often where I'd start the search.

Lakewood Village: one that confuses people. It was once part of Lakewood and was annexed into Long Beach, so it has a Long Beach address and Long Beach city services. If you're shopping both cities, read my Lakewood guide too, and the Cypress guide if Orange County is on the table.

Downtown and Alamitos Beach round it out with condos near the Queen Mary, the Aquarium of the Pacific and the A Line, which runs straight to downtown LA. Schools: Long Beach Unified serves most of the city, and the names families ask me about most are Long Beach Polytechnic High School, Wilson High School and Prisk Elementary School. Always confirm the school for a specific address with the district before you write an offer.

FAQ

Long Beach real estate questions

How much does a home cost in Long Beach, CA?

The Long Beach median is around $833K, with homes averaging about 42 days on market, per my 2026 Long Beach guide. The spread is wide. Condos downtown and homes in North Long Beach usually sit below that number, and Naples and Belmont Shore sit well above it. For your exact home, a free valuation is the honest answer.

Which Long Beach neighborhood is best for families?

It depends on what you trade. If you want older homes with character and yards, look at Bixby Knolls and California Heights. If you want a quieter ranch-home street near parks, look at Los Altos on the east side. If budget comes first, start in North Long Beach. Check the assigned Long Beach Unified school for each address.

Is Lakewood Village in Long Beach or Lakewood?

Long Beach. Lakewood Village was part of Lakewood before it was annexed into Long Beach, so homes there have Long Beach addresses and city services. The name trips up a lot of buyers comparing the two cities, so I check city limits on every listing we look at.

How long does it take to sell a house in Long Beach?

Homes average about 42 days on market citywide, per my 2026 guide. Well-priced homes in popular pockets often go faster, and overpriced homes sit longer and usually sell for less after a cut. Pricing right from day one is the biggest thing you control.

How do I choose a real estate agent in Long Beach?

Ask three things. Can they name recent sales in your neighborhood, not just the city? Who actually answers the phone? And can they show real reviews?
