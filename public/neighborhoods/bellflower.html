The place

The Story of
Bellflower.

Bellflower is a city of about 75,716 people in Los Angeles County.

Its official start date is 1957, about 69 years ago. Riverview Park, about 16 acres, is the largest of 9 parks here. For a day out there is Carpenter House Museum.

The map shows 29 places to eat and drink, with Mexican, Coffee and American the most common. There are 8 public schools inside the boundary, run by Bellflower Unified. On price, Bellflower is the 43rd most affordable of 122 cities on my map at about $793K for the typical home, and values are up 88% in ten years.

Why Bellflower

What Makes Bellflower
Worth Calling Home.

💵

Priced Below Its Neighbors

The median sale price was $822K in the three months ending October 2026 (Redfin), below Lakewood, Downey and Cerritos next door. First-time buyers actually have a shot here.

🛣️

91 + 605 + 105 Access

Three freeways within minutes. Long Beach in 15, Downtown in 25, OC in 10.

🍽️

Bellflower Blvd Renaissance

The downtown strip has been quietly getting better, new coffee shops, breweries, and a real sense of identity.

🏥

Close to Major Medical Jobs

Kaiser Permanente's Downey Medical Center sits just over the city line, one of the biggest employers in the area and a steady source of buyer demand.

Where to Live
in Bellflower.

A real-talk breakdown of the Bellflower sub-areas, from premium zones to value plays.

📍

Bellflower Boulevard / Downtown

Walkable strip with mid-rise townhomes and a few condo conversions. Best for low-maintenance buyers.

📍

North Bellflower

Older SFR stock, mid-century, larger lots. Best for first-time buyers willing to update.

📍

Mayfair / Lakewood border

Quieter, slight premium for proximity to Lakewood schools and amenities.

📍

West Bellflower, near Paramount

Most affordable corner of the market. Investor-friendly with solid rental yields.
