The Story of
South Pasadena.

About 25,656 people call South Pasadena home. It sits in Los Angeles County.

It dates to 1888. San Pascual Park, about 13 acres, is the largest of 9 parks here. Golfers have Arroyo Seco Golf Course.

Places people go: South Pasadena Historic Museum, SugarMynt Gallery and The Century House. The map shows 36 places to eat and drink, with Coffee, American and Pizza the most common. There are 5 public schools inside the boundary, run by South Pasadena Unified. On price, South Pasadena is the 12th highest-priced of 122 cities on my map at about $1.68M for the typical home, and values are up 102% in ten years.

What is the typical home value in South Pasadena, CA?

As of September 2026, the typical home in South Pasadena is worth about $1,676,432, based on the Zillow Home Value Index. Prices vary a lot by street, lot size and condition, so ask for a valuation of your specific home.

Are home values in South Pasadena going up or down?

The typical home value in South Pasadena is up 4.6% over the past year, from about $1,602,638 to about $1,676,432. Over ten years it is up 102%.

How much is a 3-bedroom home in South Pasadena?

Typical values in South Pasadena by bedroom count: 1 bedroom about $743,717, 2 bedroom about $1,094,346, 3 bedroom about $1,650,898, 4 bedroom about $2,213,853, 5+ bedroom about $2,750,587. Source: Zillow Home Value Index, September 2026.

What is the typical rent in South Pasadena?

The typical asking rent in South Pasadena is about $2,649 a month as of September 2026, based on the Zillow Observed Rent Index.

How many public schools are in South Pasadena?

5 public schools sit inside the South Pasadena boundary: 3 elementary, 1 middle and 1 high. They belong to South Pasadena Unified. School assignment depends on the home address, so confirm with the district.

What are the biggest parks in South Pasadena?

South Pasadena has 9 parks. The largest are San Pascual Park (about 13 acres), Arroyo Park (about 11 acres) and Garfield Park (about 7.6 acres). Sizes are measured from OpenStreetMap.
