Buying a home in Pico Rivera

Pico Rivera is one of the most reachable places in Southeast LA to own a house with a yard. The buyers who win here are pre-approved, know the school lines and check the details other buyers skip.

Confirm the school district

Most of the city is in El Rancho Unified, home to El Rancho High School, Rivera Middle School and Birney Tech Academy. The Montebello Gardens area is in Montebello Unified instead. I check the district for every address before you offer.

Check the map before escrow

The city sits between the Rio Hondo and the San Gabriel River. I pull the FEMA flood map and an insurance quote early for every home, so a surprise premium never shows up in week three of escrow.

Local Knowledge

Pico Rivera, area by area

Pico Rivera started as two farm towns, Pico to the north and Rivera to the south, and became one city in 1958. You can still feel that split on the map.

The housing stock. After World War II, builders came for the open farmland, and most of the city filled in with postwar tract homes. That makes Pico Rivera a good place to compare apples to apples, and a tough place to hide an overpriced listing.

The north side. The old Pico community runs up toward the Whittier Narrows, and the Pico Rivera Sports Arena is up here. One pocket to know about anywhere in town: the Montebello Gardens area sends kids to Montebello Unified, not El Rancho. Buyers with school plans need to know that before they fall for a house.

The middle of town. Rosemead Boulevard (Route 19) runs straight down the center, and Smith Park sits right on it. Homes within a few blocks of the park and the El Rancho schools draw families first.

The south side. The old Rivera community makes up the south end of town, down toward Telegraph Road. Up on Washington Boulevard, the Pico Rivera Towne Center stands where the Ford assembly plant and later Northrop's B-2 bomber work once were. Today that means groceries and errands close to home.

The river edges. The Rio Hondo bounds the west side and the San Gabriel River the east, with county bike paths along both. If you ride or run, a home near a trail access point is a real lifestyle upgrade.

Commute. The 605 runs along the southeast edge of town and links you to the 5 and the 60. Rosemead, Paramount and Passons Boulevard carry most north south traffic. If you work in Commerce or downtown LA, test Washington Boulevard at 5pm before you decide.

Want to compare? Pico Rivera's neighbors Downey and Montebello are close in feel and very different in price. One thing to do this week: look up the school district for the two addresses you like most, then drive both at 5pm on a weekday. Selling instead? Start with a free valuation of your home.

FAQ

Pico Rivera real estate questions

How much does a home cost in Pico Rivera, CA?

The median Pico Rivera home sells for around $739K and takes about 45 days to go under contract, based on my Pico Rivera city guide. That is below nearby Downey and Whittier. Lot size, condition and permitted additions move the price the most, so I price every home off its closest true comps.

Can I buy a home in Pico Rivera with an ITIN?

Yes. ITIN loans are legal in California and are made by lenders who specialize in them. They usually need 10% to 20% down and carry a higher rate than a standard loan. Carlos used one to buy a $635K Pico Rivera home, 28 days from offer to keys. Talk to a CPA or attorney about any tax or legal questions.

Which school district serves Pico Rivera?

Most of Pico Rivera is in El Rancho Unified, which includes El Rancho High School, Rivera Middle School, Birney Tech Academy and Rivera Elementary School. The Montebello Gardens area is in Montebello Unified. Always confirm the district for the exact address before you write an offer.

How do I choose a real estate agent in Pico Rivera?
