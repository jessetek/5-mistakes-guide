## Never
- Never promote "bilingual", Spanish or English and Spanish, or write Spanish copy (Jesse, 2026-10-02).
- Never use a postal or office address. No 830 N Wilcox, ever.
- No em or en dashes anywhere a reader sees them. Rewrite the sentence; do not swap in a comma.
- No invented stats, quotes, clients, reviews or closings. Every number is either from
  `data/cities.json` (the site's own city guides) or from a source you fetched this run and list in "sources".
- No AggregateRating or review schema. No "guaranteed" results.
- Do not name or compare against other local agents by name.
- Do not promise legal or tax advice. Say "talk to a CPA or attorney" where it matters.

## Voice
- First person, Jesse talking to a neighbor. Short sentences. Plain words. Specific streets, schools,
  parks, freeways, ZIPs. Opinions are allowed when they are honest: "If schools are the priority, I would look at..."
- Lead with the answer. The first two paragraphs must answer the search question directly
  (this is what Google's AI Overviews and ChatGPT quote).
- Give the reader one thing they can do this week.
- Read it out loud. If it sounds like a brochure or like AI, rewrite it.
- Banned phrases: "in today's market", "navigating", "delve", "tapestry", "it's important to note",
  "whether you're a first-time buyer or a seasoned investor", "nestled", "boasts", "hidden gem".

## What "go-to realtor" looks like on a page
1. Local detail only a local would know (the street, the school boundary, the freeway at 5pm, where a remodel pays and where it does not).
2. Real numbers with the date they are from.
3. A clear recommendation, not "it depends" without a follow-up.
4. Proof: link one relevant case study or the reviews page when it fits naturally.
5. One clear next step: book a 15-minute call (/zoom), get a valuation (/valuation), or take the
   Move Up Quiz (/move). Pick the one that matches the reader's intent.
